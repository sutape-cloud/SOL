GLOBAL STYLE
Live-action microdrama, 16:9. Real people, real places, natural skin. Four shots, 20 seconds, at real-time speed.
All the acting is natural and real, at the size of real life: small and natural. Feelings show quietly. The only light is the low orange-gold sun and its warm reflection off the glass of the buildings; the only lens flare comes from the real sun. The air is clear and clean. Every surface is plain. Every person has two arms and two legs, natural and correct. The camera keeps one fixed focal length per shot. Whenever Aoy smiles, it is a closed-lip smile. The only sound is the real sound of the place.

LIP SYNC
Win speaks in Shot 1, Aoy answers in Shot 2, and Win speaks again in Shot 3. Each line is lip-synced to the person saying it. The voice files play in order, each exactly once: @Audio1 in Shot 1, @Audio2 in Shot 2, @Audio3 in Shot 3. A voice file may end with a stretch of silence that is there only to make the file long enough; only the spoken words are used, and the acting carries straight on to the next beat. Everything ends within 20 seconds.

SCENE
Win is so relieved that Aoy got out safely that he covers it with a joke: with a straight face he asks whether she has been hitting someone with that staff. Aoy laughs, says Nuch gave it to her, and then, all curious and full of admiration, asks how he knows Nuch, who was amazing. Win cannot tell her the truth, so he makes up a story on the spot: Nuch used to run the motorbike taxi stand near his home. He is a bad liar, but Aoy believes every word, and Win quietly breathes out, relieved.
