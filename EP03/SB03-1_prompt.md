GLOBAL STYLE
Live-action microdrama, 16:9. Real people, real places, natural skin. Eight shots, 28 seconds, at real-time speed.
All the acting is natural and real, at the size of real life, like real people on an ordinary working afternoon: small, simple and unhurried. Feelings show quietly. The air is clear and clean. The only light is the afternoon sun through the half-open vertical blinds, the downlights in the ceiling, the brass table lamp, the glow of the TV screen and the corridor light through the narrow glass panel beside the door. One warm, soft afternoon-office grade for the whole video. Every surface in the room is plain. The camera keeps one fixed focal length per shot. The only sound is the real sound of the room.

Nuch speaks in Shot 3, Shot 6 and Shot 8. Win speaks in Shot 4, Shot 5 and Shot 7. Each line is lip-synced to the person saying it. All of this has already happened before the first frame. This is the first video of a new scene. Nuch is his secretary of many years, the one person he goes to when he needs help with Aoy. It is three o'clock in the afternoon, and Nuch is in the small meeting room of the secretary team, listening to two young assistants present their slides.

SCENE
Win needs Nuch's help with Aoy again, and he is embarrassed to ask, so he hovers outside the meeting room and peeks in through the narrow glass panel beside the door instead of walking in. Nuch spots him and knows at once what he has come about. She calls him in with perfect professional politeness. Win only says her name and glances at the assistants, because this has to stay between the two of them, and Nuch sends the assistants out with a nod. Alone with her, Win admits he has come to trouble her again; Nuch guesses that it is about Aoy, with amused affection, and he admits it. She puts everything else aside and gets ready to help him at once.
