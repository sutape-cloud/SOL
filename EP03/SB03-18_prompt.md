GLOBAL STYLE
Live-action microdrama, 16:9. Real people, real places, natural skin. Six shots, 22 seconds, at real-time speed.
All the acting is natural and real, at the size of real life: small and natural. Feelings show quietly. The only light is the low orange-gold sun and its warm reflection off the glass of the buildings and the white railings; the only lens flare comes from the real sun. The air is clear and clean. Every surface is plain. Every person has two arms and two legs, natural and correct. Each shot keeps one fixed focal length. Whenever Aoy smiles, it is a closed-lip smile. The only sound is the real sound of the place.

LIP SYNC
Aoy speaks in Shot 1 and again in Shot 4, and Win speaks in Shot 5. Each line is lip-synced to the person saying it. The voice files play in order, each exactly once: @Audio1 in Shot 1, @Audio2 in Shot 4, @Audio3 in Shot 5. A voice file may end with a stretch of silence that is there only to make the file long enough; only the spoken words are used, and the acting carries straight on to the next beat. Everything ends within 22 seconds.

SCENE
Leaning on the railing above the traffic, Aoy opens up: she is glad Nuch came to get her out, because if Win had gone in himself, those people would have kept her there. She is still a little scared when she thinks of it, and relieved it is over. Instead of the road, Win is looking at her, longer than he should. She catches him, looks away, shy, then finds the courage to turn back and thank him, from the heart. Win, touched and embarrassed, hides it with a cheeky joke: with a straight face he pretends something is stuck on his cheek and offers it to her, clearly asking for a kiss on the cheek. She gets it at once, slaps his arm and turns away, laughing and shy, while he rubs his arm and keeps smiling at her.
